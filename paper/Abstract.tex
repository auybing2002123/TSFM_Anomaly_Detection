\begin{abstract}
Cloud microservice systems have become the backbone of modern online services, where timely fault diagnosis is critical for maintaining reliability and service availability. However, \lb{online diagnosis under production-like replay settings} remains challenging due to three fundamental issues: heterogeneous multimodal monitoring data, \lb{deadline-driven latency requirements} for online inference, and dynamic workload variations that render static diagnosis pipelines inefficient.
To address these challenges, this paper presents \textbf{ASID}, an \textit{\textbf{A}daptive \textbf{S}parse \textbf{I}nference framework for online multimodal fault \textbf{D}iagnosis} in cloud microservice systems. ASID first encodes metrics, logs, and traces through modality-specific lightweight encoders and projects them into a unified temporal representation space. It then employs a deviation-aware temporal backbone to predict system evolution and extract anomaly-sensitive residual representations. To reduce online inference overhead, ASID introduces a service-aware sparse expert routing mechanism \lb{with a confidence-adaptive budget, activating up to two lightweight adapters inside the temporal backbone and avoiding unnecessary expert evaluations on confident windows}.
\lb{We evaluate ASID on the Eadro-SN benchmark under full online replay. ASID achieves F1\,=\,0.9838 with zero deadline misses under a 25\,ms tight deadline and p99 latency of 16.69\,ms. Compared with XGBoost evaluated under the same full replay, ASID improves F1 by 5.76 percentage points. Supplementary anomaly-detection checks on MSDS and RE2-TT provide cross-dataset evidence, and a separate RCAEval RE2-TT study examines whether the learned deviation representation supports service-level diagnostic ranking. These results show that adaptive sparse inference provides a practical accuracy--latency trade-off for deadline-aware intelligent cloud operations.}
\end{abstract}
